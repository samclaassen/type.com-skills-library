Subject: The handoff that breaks every project

Projects rarely fail at the start or the end. They fail in the handoff between strategy and production, when the person who sold the idea leaves the room.

## Handoffs need a written brief, not a meeting

A handoff meeting feels efficient. Then the producer remembers half of it and the designer remembers the other half. A one-page brief, written by the strategist and signed off by the producer, costs 20 minutes and saves a week of rework.

**Steal this:** no task moves to production without a linked brief.

## Name one owner per client, not per project

Clients don't care about your org chart. They want one person who answers. When three project managers each own a slice, the client emails all three and trusts none of them.

**Steal this:** assign a single account owner and route every client email through them.
